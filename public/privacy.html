Privacy notice

What Matchday keeps, where, who can see it, and how to delete it. Last updated [date published].

The short version
  • Most of Matchday lives on your phone. With an account, the player's records are also kept on a database so your family's phones stay in step.
  • Nobody else sees them unless you invite family, or say yes to an academy. You can leave an academy at any time.
  • Match photos and videos never leave the phone they're on - unless you choose a clip for the coach to watch.
  • No adverts, no analytics, no tracking, no selling data. Ever.
  • Nobody under 13 has an account of their own: a parent or guardian signs in for them.
  • Delete your account from the app - Setup → Sharing with your family → Delete my account - and it and everything stored with it is deleted straight away.

1. Who we are

Matchday is a football tracker for one player's matches, training and stats, with family sharing and academies. It is run by Aarav Chopra ("we"), who decides how your information is used (the "controller"). Contact: [contact email].

2. On your phone

Everything you enter is kept in this browser on this phone, so the app works with no signal: the player's name, date of birth, age group, position and photo; teams, competitions, matches (opponent, date, ground, score, your stats and notes), training, other teams' results for tables, and your settings. It stays there until you delete it.

Also only on the phone: match photos and videos, the coach's conversation, any personal AI key you add, and your answer to the age question.

This is the browser's own storage, used only to make the app work. Matchday sets no cookies and nothing on the phone is used to track you.

3. Your account

Signing in keeps the player's records safe if a phone is lost, and shares them with your family's phones.

  • Your email address is how you sign in: we email a six-digit code to it each time a new phone signs in. Nobody in the app sees it except people in your own family. Academies and other players only ever see a username you choose.
  • The sign-in service (Supabase, see section 9) also records when you sign in, and the IP address and type of device each sign-in came from. That's how it keeps accounts secure.
  • Sign-in emails are sent through [Google's Gmail / Brevo - whichever is set up to send Matchday's emails], which handles your email address and the code to deliver them.
  • Google sign-in, where it's switched on, is optional. Google shares your name, email and profile picture with the sign-in service; Matchday uses only your email.
  • Your age band. Before signing in, Matchday asks the month and year you were born. That answer stays on the phone. The account only records whether its holder is 13-17 or 18 or over - never the date.

4. What the server keeps

With an account, these are kept on our database as well as on the phone:

  • the player's profile - name, date of birth, age group, position and photo;
  • their teams, competitions, matches (with notes), training, other teams' results and the app's settings;
  • your family: who is in it, and invitations by email;
  • your username, if you pick one, and your age band (above).

Everyone in your family can see and change the player's records. Nobody else can - the database refuses them. We test that it does.

When you delete a single match or session, it disappears from every phone. On the server it is kept, marked as deleted, so that phones that were offline learn it has gone. It is removed for good when the account is deleted.

Not on the server: match photos and videos, the coach's conversation, AI keys, and the month and year from the age question.

5. Academies

An academy sees a player's records only after their family says yes - by accepting an invite, or asking to join with the academy's code. Before that, an academy that invites a player knows only the username it typed in. For a player under 13, only a parent or guardian, signed in with their own email, can say yes. We record who said yes, and when.

Once linked, academy staff can read - never change - the player's profile (including photo and date of birth), matches with their notes, training and stats, as far as their role allows. The academy also keeps its own records: its squads, its competitions and results, team selections, and the player's name on its list.

Leaving an academy (Setup → Academies → Leave) stops it seeing anything straight away. It keeps the player's name on its list, marked as left. Squad mates see each other's names only.

Academies send Matchday registration and certificate documents to be verified. Only the academy's owner and Matchday's admins can see them, and they are deleted as soon as they have been checked.

6. The coach and reading schedules

These are the only features that send what you choose to an AI service. Nothing is sent unless you use them, and they are off for anyone under 13 until a parent or guardian has signed in.

  • What is sent: the player's position and age group (never their name); what you ask for; for a clip, the video or still frames from it, your description of which player you are and any context you add; for a schedule, the photo, screenshot or PDF, and the names of your teams.
  • Where it goes: to Matchday's small server on Cloudflare, which passes it to Google's Gemini API and returns the answer. Matchday's server does not keep it.
  • What Google does with it: Google's terms for the Gemini API apply. [Check which applies to Matchday's key and keep one: "On Google's free tier, Google may use what it receives to improve its products, and people at Google may read it." / "Matchday uses Google's paid service, which does not use what it receives to improve Google's products."] Please don't send clips you would not want seen this way.
  • Your own key (Setup → AI): if you add one, requests go straight from your phone to Google (Gemini) or Anthropic (Claude), under the terms of your own account with them. Matchday never sees your key.

To stop the shared coach being overused, Matchday's server counts requests per day by a scrambled form of your IP address (never the address itself), and forgets the count within two days.

7. Match reminders

If you switch them on (Setup → Match reminders), the phone sends Matchday's server on Cloudflare its next few reminders - such as "Tomorrow: @ Oakfield · Kick-off 10:30 AM · Riverside Park" - and a push address the phone's maker gives it. Each reminder is deleted once sent; once there are none left, the push address is deleted too. Switching reminders off deletes both.

Reminders reach the phone through its maker's push service - Google (Chrome and Android), Apple, Mozilla (Firefox) or Microsoft (Edge). The message is encrypted so that only your phone can read it; the push service only sees that a message was sent.

8. Maps, sharing and hosting

  • Directions: tapping a ground opens Apple Maps (iPhone, iPad, Mac) or Google Maps, searching for the ground's name. Nothing is sent until you tap.
  • Sharing a match card, tournament or calendar entry uses your phone's own share sheet. It goes only where you send it.
  • Hosting: the app is served by GitHub Pages. Like any website host, GitHub records the IP address of devices that load it, for security.
  • Fonts come with the app; nothing is loaded from font services.

10. Children

Most Matchday players are children, so the app is built around that.

  • Nobody under 13 has their own account. Before any sign-in, Matchday asks whoever is holding the phone when they were born. Someone under 13 is asked to fetch a parent or guardian, who signs in with their own email - the code goes to them - and agrees to this notice first.
  • Until a parent or guardian has signed in, an under-13 can still use Matchday on the phone only: nothing they enter leaves it, and the coach and reminders stay off.
  • Only a parent or guardian can say yes to an academy for a player under 13. A player with no date of birth is treated as under 13.
  • Everything is private by default: nothing is shared until a family chooses to share it, and the app never nudges anyone to share more.
  • No adverts, no tracking, no profiling, and nothing about a child is sold or used for marketing.

11. What we never do

No adverts. No analytics or tracking - not Google Analytics, not the Meta pixel, nothing - and no cookies. No selling or renting data. No profiling, and no decisions made about anyone by a computer alone. (The coach's ratings are advice for the player, nothing more.)

14. Your rights, and deleting

Delete your account in the app: Setup → Sharing with your family → Delete my account. It deletes your email address, username and age band. If nobody else is in your family - or you started it and choose "Everything" - it also deletes the player's profile, photo and every record, and any linked academy loses sight of them at once. It clears the phone too. To keep a copy first, use Setup → Your data → Export backup.

An academy's owner deletes the academy from its settings; an owner who has other staff hands it over first.

You can also ask us to see, correct or delete what we hold about you or your child, to stop using it, or for a copy you can take elsewhere, by emailing [contact email]. We will answer within a month. You can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk - though we'd like the chance to put it right first.

15. Changes

If this notice changes in a way that matters, the app will say so before the change applies.
